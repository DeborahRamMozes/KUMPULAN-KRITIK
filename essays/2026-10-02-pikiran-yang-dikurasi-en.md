# CURATED THOUGHT

## When a Critical Text Is Called “Still a Draft” After Its Critique Begins to Disturb

### Bilingual edition - BMHS + Sudjojono Human Flow Protocol

There is one word that seems very simple in the academic world: draft. The word usually means that a text is unfinished, an argument is not yet fully built, the data is insufficient, or the structure still requires revision. Yet, like many other words in the worlds of art, academia, and cultural institutions, draft can change function when it comes from someone who possesses authority. It no longer merely indicates the working stage of a text. It can become a marker that the text is not yet considered legitimate enough to stand on its own. The problem, then, is no longer simply whether a text is unfinished, but who has the right to determine when a thought is considered finished.

### The Real Question Is Not “Why Was My Text Called a Draft?”

The question is simpler and more disturbing: when was this text declared to be a draft? Was that status communicated to the writer from the beginning of the mentoring process? Were there clear criteria identifying which parts were unfinished? Had the mentor previously provided substantive corrections? Or did the term draft acquire its force only after the text revealed a critique that might make some people uncomfortable? This question does not arise because a writer rejects criticism. Academic writing must indeed be readable, testable, refutable, revisable, and, when evidence is insufficient, capable of having its argument dismantled. Criticism of criticism is part of the work of knowledge. What is not acceptable is using the word draft without specifying what exactly is being evaluated: its structure, methodology, evidence, language, or perhaps the courage of its argument.

### Who Curates Thought?

Within the visual-art ecosystem, we speak too often about who curates artworks and too rarely ask who curates the thinking that speaks about those artworks. We know exhibition curators. We know institutional curators. We know editors. We know mentors. We know reviewers. All hold legitimate functions in the production of knowledge. Yet each function also carries a position of power. It determines what is relevant, what is too harsh, what is immature, what is academic, what is emotional, what must be softened, and ultimately what can enter the space of knowledge with a body already combed and disciplined into neatness.

### Critical Thought Is Not Born Neat

We too often want criticism to arrive as a finished object: clean, polite, systematic, disturbing no one. Yet critical thought usually begins because something scratches. There is a contradiction. There is disorder. There is a relationship that has been treated as normal for too long. There is a name that always appears. There is a name that never appears. There is an institution that always becomes a door. There is an individual who remains outside the door. There is a language used so continuously that everyone forgets to ask who first made that language into a norm.

### THE ITCHSM: Itching as Method

THE ITCHSM works from precisely that kind of itch. Not itch as injured feeling. Not itch as an excuse to accuse. Itch is a symptom that something has not yet been fully read. When a critical text discovers something disturbing, the academic response should not first be to wrap it in an etiquette of politeness. The first response should be: prove it. Where is the data? What is the source? How did the relation form? When did it happen? Who was involved? What can be verified? What remains an indication? What is still unknown? What can be refuted? These questions make criticism stronger. Criticism does not become weak because it is tested. It becomes weak when it refuses to be tested.

### Criticism Is Not Academic Decoration

There is a strange tendency in some academic art environments: criticism is accepted as long as it still sounds like criticism. It may be sharp, but not too sharp. It may question institutions, but not enough to make the institution's name felt. It may discuss power, but not enough for the power relation to be traceable. It may discuss conflicts of interest, but preferably in language so soft that the conflict loses its shape. In the end, criticism becomes academic decoration.

### Tanah Seni, Tanah Rupa, Tubuh Seni, Tubuh Rupa

Tanah Seni asks about infrastructure: institutions, economy, history, language, attention, exhibitions, archives, markets, patronage, reputation, and platforms that allow something to obtain the status of art. Tanah Rupa reads the material and visual conditions in which form appears: objects, surfaces, spaces, materials, signs, bodies, memory, environment, geography, and visual language. Tubuh Seni reads the social body and the systems that determine how form is seen, valued, circulated, stored, and remembered: experience, culture, education, friendship, kinship, work, knowledge, reputation, and collective relations. Tubuh Rupa reads the place where those pressures take form: the individual, artwork, medium, material, technique, process, visual grammar, and embodiment.

### Who Curates Thought?

If artworks may be curated, then art writing may certainly be edited. But there is a fundamental difference between editing an argument and editing the courage of an argument. The first is academic work. The second is a question of power. An editor can say: the data does not yet support your claim. That can be examined. An editor can say: the causal relationship has not been demonstrated. That can be tested. An editor can say: a primary source is not yet available. That can be sought. But when a text must be softened because its critique may offend someone, the problem has changed.

### Sudjojono and the Courage to Look

In Sudjojono’s line of thought, art is never sufficient as beautiful surface alone. There is the question of jiwa ketok, of what actually becomes visible beneath form, of honesty of experience, social reality, and the artist’s attitude toward the world confronted. That spirit matters because art criticism today can become too busy producing language that appears critical instead of doing the work of looking. Language can lie. Data is harder. Digital traces are more stubborn. Archives are more irritating.

### Collective Does Not Automatically Mean Open

The word collective has an excellent moral reputation. It sounds egalitarian. It sounds together. It sounds horizontal. But a structure does not become open merely because it uses a word that sounds open. Collectives also have doors. There are members. There are outsiders. There is a core. There is a periphery. There are people who know insiders. There are people who receive invitations. There are people who know when a project is being designed. There are people who know nothing until the project is finished and its photographs have already circulated on social media.

### When “Draft” Becomes a Door

Perhaps the text really is still a draft. Then say which part is still a draft. Show it. Prove it. Edit it together. Revise it. I have no problem with that. The problem begins when the word draft arrives without anatomy. Draft where? Which argument? Which data? Which methodology? Which language? Which references? Which structure? Without that, draft is no longer a diagnosis. It is merely a label. And labeling is one of the simplest forms of power in institutions of knowledge.

### A Criticism That Is Too Neat Is Another Problem

I do not believe criticism must always be harsh. But I also do not believe criticism must always be polite. Politeness is not a measure of truth. Academic is not a synonym for gentle. Harsh language can be wrong. Polite language can also be wrong. Highly academic language can even conceal error beautifully. Therefore the measure of criticism is not whether it is pleasant to read. The measure is whether it can be held accountable.

### Thought Should Not Be Turned into a Finished Commodity

Writing must retain room to move. It encounters new data, discards hypotheses, repairs terminology, dismantles assumptions, finds contradictions, and sometimes discovers that its original question was wrong. That is precisely where research becomes alive. Therefore I do not reject editorial process. I reject editorial intervention that is not transparent about what exactly is being edited. I do not reject mentors. I reject the mentor position when the basis of intervention is not explained. I do not reject correction. I reject correction that cannot be distinguished from a request to domesticate critique.

### Back to Art: The Problem Is Always the Door

Indonesian visual art has had a problem with doors for too long: the gallery door, the biennale door, the museum door, the curator’s door, the collector’s door, the residency door, the funding door, the education door, the network door, the reputation door. Then we make collectives to say that these doors should be opened. But history has a bad habit: it makes people reproduce the structure they have just criticized. Therefore my question does not stop at who owns the door. I want to see how the door works.

### Conclusion: The Winner Always Writes the Story

In the end, the question of draft is not about one word. It reaches a larger question: who has the right to determine the form of legitimate knowledge? Who decides that a text is mature? Who decides that a critique is too harsh? Who determines the proper language? Who decides what counts as academic? Who decides what is objective? And who decides who is allowed to ask? In art history, the winner often writes the story. But criticism has another task: to open the archive closed by that story. Not to replace one winner with another, but to examine its soil, read its bodies, trace its forms, and follow its web. Criticism is not afraid of being wrong. Criticism is afraid of being denied the possibility of testing its own wrongness.

## PROTOCOL NOTE

- BMHS authorial flow: preserve the central question, argumentative tension, distinct terminology, and paragraph movement without flattening the voice into generic academic language.
- Sudjojono Human Flow: foreground experience, social reality, courage to look, and the relation among form, reality, and attitude.
- Evidence discipline: claims about institutional, family, collective, or selection relations must be verified through sources and separated from hypotheses.
- Mandatory ontological order: TANAH SENI -> TANAH RUPA -> TUBUH SENI -> TUBUH RUPA.
